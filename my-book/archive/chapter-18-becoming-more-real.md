# Chapter 18: Becoming More Real

**STATUS: DRAFT**

## Section 1: Opening

There is a sentence buried in the Urantia Book's discussion of spiritual growth that changes how you read everything else. It is easy to miss because it is short, and because what it says is the opposite of what most people expect from a spiritual teaching:

> "Persons become more real as they ascend from the seventh to the first level of mortal existence." (UB 110:6.10)

More real. Not less attached. Not more detached. Not more ethereal, not more abstract, not more "spiritual" in the floating, disembodied sense the word usually carries. More real. The further you travel on the spiritual path, the more genuinely, solidly, concretely yourself you become. You do not dissolve into God. You crystallize into the person God always meant you to be.

The Urantia Book calls these levels of growth the psychic circles, seven stages of "mortal potentiality" that every human being traverses on the way from first moral choice to full partnership with the divine spirit within:

> "The sum total of personality realization on a material world is contained within the successive conquest of the seven psychic circles of mortal potentiality." (UB 110:6.1)

And these circles are not about becoming smarter or holier or more disciplined in any narrow sense. They encompass everything a person is:

> "The psychic circles are not exclusively intellectual, neither are they wholly morontial; they have to do with personality status, mind attainment, soul growth, and Adjuster attunement." (UB 110:6.3)

Personality status. Mind attainment. Soul growth. Adjuster attunement. The whole person, growing in every dimension at once. This is the Urantia Book's map of the journey from time to eternity: not a straight line upward, but a progressive expansion of what it means to be you.

A Course in Miracles describes the same journey from the other end. Where the Urantia Book provides the map, the Course provides the wake-up call:

> "You are at home in God, dreaming of exile, but perfectly capable of awakening to Reality." (ACIM, T 9 H 6)

You are already home. You are dreaming that you are not. And the journey the circles describe is the process of waking up from that dream, not all at once, not in a blinding flash, but gradually, one decision at a time, becoming more real with each step.

The Course speaks from the destination. From eternity looking back, the dream is not real. That is the view from the mountaintop. The Urantia Book speaks from the trail. Here are the levels you will pass through, and at each one, what counts as "real" shifts. Both are telling the truth. They are simply standing in different places.

And there is one person who walked the entire trail, from the seventh circle to beyond the first, while living in the flesh. One person whose biography we have in extraordinary detail. One person who then, from the vantage point of completion, dictated a teaching manual describing what he learned.

His name was Jesus. The Urantia Book tells the story of his life. The Course tells us what it meant. Together, they reveal a single journey: a human being becoming more real, year by year, circle by circle, until he was the most real person who ever lived.

## Section 2: The Outer Circles

On February 11, 2 B.C., when Jesus was not yet five years old, something happened that would shape the rest of human history. It happened without anyone noticing.

> "There came to abide with him a Thought Adjuster, a divine gift of the Paradise Father... Jesus was no more aware of the coming of the divine Monitor than are the millions upon millions of other children who, before and since that day, have likewise received these Thought Adjusters to indwell their minds and work for the ultimate spiritualization of these minds and the eternal survival of their evolving immortal souls." (UB 123:2.1)

This is the seventh circle. Entry. The moment moral choice becomes possible and the divine fragment arrives. Every normal child on every inhabited world passes through this threshold. Jesus passed through it exactly the way everyone else does: without knowing.

Course readers know this presence by a different name. What the Urantia Book calls the Thought Adjuster, the Course calls the Soul: the divine gift God placed in every mind, the presence that is "already in you" and "true forever." The language differs but the reality is the same. The Urantia Book simply tells us when it arrived, and what it did next.

And Jesus, in the Course, tells us what it meant to him:

> "I was a man who remembered the Soul and its knowledge, and as a man I did not attempt to counteract error with knowledge so much as to correct error from the bottom up. I demonstrated both the powerlessness of the body and the power of the mind. By uniting my will with that of my Creator, I naturally remembered the Soul and its own real purpose." (JCIM, T 3 F 21)

A man who remembered the Soul. Not a God who never forgot. The entire biography that follows is the story of how he remembered: slowly, one decision at a time, from the bottom up. The Soul arrived at age five. It would take twenty-five years of living before Jesus could say he had fully remembered what it carried.

At six, the boy was already different in ways his parents could not quite explain. He prayed, as all Jewish children prayed, but he insisted on adding something of his own:

> "He insisted on talking to his heavenly Father much as he would talk to Joseph, his earthly father. This departure from the more solemn and reverent modes of communication with Deity was a bit disconcerting to his parents... he would say his prayers just as he had been taught, after which he insisted on having 'just a little talk with my Father in heaven.'" (UB 123:3.6)

A child talking to God the way he would talk to his dad. No formality. No theology. Just a little talk. This is the outer circles: the first faint sense that there is something more, expressed in the only language a child has. The paradox of the journey has not yet arrived. There is no conflict between the material and the spiritual because the child does not yet know the difference. He simply feels close to his Father in heaven, the way he feels close to his father on earth.

At ten, on a Sabbath walk with Joseph, the first real stirring:

> "Jesus first gave expression to feelings and ideas which indicated that he was becoming self-conscious of the unusual nature of his life mission." (UB 124:2.1)

Both parents listened. Neither explained. They let the awareness unfold on its own schedule. This is worth noting. The greatest spiritual awakening in human history was not forced, not accelerated, not imposed. It grew naturally, at the pace a child's mind could absorb it.

At twelve came the temple. And something broke open.

The family traveled to Jerusalem for Passover. The boy had never seen the temple sacrifices. When he did, the sight of the killing and the blood overwhelmed him:

> "The bloodstained pavement, the gory hands of the priests, and the sounds of the dying animals were more than this nature-loving lad could stand. The terrible sight sickened this boy of Nazareth; he clutched his father's arm and begged to be taken away." (UB 125:1.4)

Something in him already knew this was wrong, before he had the words for it. He asked the temple teachers a question they could not answer: "If God is a father who loves his children, why all this slaughter of animals to gain divine favor?" And then, returning home, he made a declaration that marks the end of childhood and the beginning of something else entirely:

> "While I must do the will of my Father in heaven, I will also be obedient to my father on earth. I will await my hour." (UB 125:6.11)

I will await my hour. Twelve years old, already sensing a mission he could not see clearly, already choosing obedience to something he could feel but not yet name. The circle research maps this period as the transition from the outer circles to the middle: the first self-consciousness of unusual mission, the first experience of dual-nature confusion. The Urantia Book records "flood tides of spiritual illumination" at this age. Something was accelerating.

The Course, reflecting on this process, offers a quiet summary:

> "I am the model for rebirth, but rebirth itself is merely the dawning on your minds of what is already in them. God placed it there Himself, and so it is true forever." (JCIM, T 6 B 7)

What was already in him was dawning. It would take another twenty years before the dawn became full daylight. But the journey through the outer circles was complete. He had entered the territory where the paradox begins to bite: knowing you are more than what the world tells you, and not yet knowing how to live that knowledge.

## Section 3: The Middle Circles

Here is where the story gets difficult. And here is where it becomes most useful.

The Urantia Book opens Paper 126 with a statement that reframes everything most people think they know about the temptation of Jesus:

> "These two years, after he began to be self-conscious of divinity and destiny, and before he achieved a large measure of communication with his indwelling Adjuster, were the most trying of his eventful life on Urantia. It is this period of two years which should be called the great test, the real temptation." (UB 126:0.1)

Not the forty days in the wilderness. Not Gethsemane. Ages fourteen and fifteen were the real temptation. And what made them so? He knew what he was, but he could not yet hear from within.

In the language of the circles, this is the approach to the third circle, perhaps the most painful passage in the entire journey. At the third circle, the Urantia Book says, a personal guardian of destiny is assigned. The Adjuster begins to "morontiaize" the mind. But before that threshold is crossed, there is a gap: self-conscious of divinity, unable to communicate with the Adjuster. Living in two worlds that have not yet learned to speak to each other. The paradox at this stage feels like believing two contradictory things and being unable to reconcile them.

Every student of the Course who has sensed the truth but cannot seem to hold it, who has felt the presence but cannot sustain the connection, who knows what they are supposed to be but has no idea how to get there, knows exactly what this feels like. Jesus lived in that gap for years.

And into those years, ordinary tragedy arrived with no warning.

> "All did go well until that fateful day of Tuesday, September 25, when a runner from Sepphoris brought to this Nazareth home the tragic news that Joseph had been severely injured by the falling of a derrick... But Joseph died of his injuries before Mary arrived." (UB 126:2.1)

Jesus was fourteen. His father died in a construction accident. He did not get to say goodbye. And overnight, this boy who was beginning to sense his cosmic destiny became the sole provider for a widowed mother, seven younger siblings, and one child not yet born:

> "This carpenter lad, now just past fourteen years of age, awakened to the realization that he had not only to fulfill the commission of his heavenly Father to reveal the divine nature on earth and in the flesh, but that his young human nature must also shoulder the responsibility of caring for his widowed mother and seven brothers and sisters." (UB 126:2.2)

Two obligations. One cosmic, one deeply ordinary. And no guidance from within on how to hold them both. This was not an exercise in spiritual detachment. This was a teenager picking up a hammer and going to work because the family needed to eat, while carrying inside himself the knowledge that he was supposed to transform the world.

The Course gives us a glimpse of how Jesus, looking back, understood this period:

> "I could not understand their importance to YOU if I had not once been tempted to believe in them myself." (JCIM, T 4 B 34)

He was tempted. Not by the devil on a mountaintop. By the same things that tempt us: exhaustion, grief, the weight of responsibility, the apparent reality of the material world. He had to be tempted by these things, or he could not teach us how to move through them.

The Course says the world is a dream. The Urantia Book does not disagree, exactly, but it adds a critical nuance: what seems illusory from the absolute level may be absolutely real on the finite level. A fourteen-year-old boy burying his father is not having an illusion. He is having a human experience. The growth through the circles does not require denying that experience. It requires navigating it with increasing partnership between the human mind and the divine presence within.

And that is what Jesus did. At seventeen, something shifted:

> "Jesus made great progress in the organization of his mind. Gradually he had brought his divine and human natures together, and he accomplished all this organization of intellect by the force of his own *decisions* and with only the aid of his indwelling Monitor, just such a Monitor as all normal mortals on all postbestowal-Son worlds have within their minds." (UB 127:2.12)

By the force of his own decisions. With the same type of Adjuster available to every mortal. No shortcuts. No supernatural interventions. Progress earned the same way any human earns it: one choice at a time. This is the third circle: the mind organizing itself around its true center, the two natures beginning to work together instead of in conflict.

At nineteen, a young woman named Rebecca fell in love with him. She was the daughter of a wealthy merchant. She offered him everything his struggling family needed: financial security, companionship, a normal life. He turned her down, gently but completely:

> "If I am a son of destiny, I must not assume obligations of lifelong duration until such a time as my destiny shall be made manifest." (UB 127:5.5)

Rebecca never married. She followed him from a distance through his entire public ministry, and she stood among the women at the cross. The Urantia Book calls him, through her eyes, "the one altogether lovely and the greatest among ten thousand."

The temptations were not only personal. They were political and financial. The Zealots wanted him to join their nationalist cause. He refused. A wealthy citizen of Damascus offered him a fortune to establish a religious school. He turned it down. At every fork, he chose the harder road: the one that required patience, that refused to force the hour, that trusted the Father's timing over every shortcut the world offered.

By twenty-one, "he had already effectively combined these two natures into one." And the method was entirely human:

> "He obtained knowledge, gained experience, and combined these into wisdom, just as do other mortals of the realm. Until after his baptism he availed himself of no supernatural power." (UB 128:1.3)

Just as do other mortals. This sentence is the bridge between his life and the Course. What he achieved, he achieved through the same means available to us: knowledge, experience, wisdom, decision. The Course, looking back on this period with remarkable honesty, acknowledges that the journey was not flawless:

> "These are not at all like the several slips into impatience which I made." (JCIM, T 6 B 15)

He made slips. The Course records it. The Urantia Book corroborates it in the story of his adolescence and young manhood: the frustrations, the doubts, the moments when the gap between what he knew and what he could do must have felt unbearable. He was not playing a role. He was living a life. The Urantia Book says plainly: "He experienced the full gamut of human feelings and emotions; he was 'in all things tested, even as you are,' and he suffered and died." The full gamut. Not a curated selection. Every feeling you have ever felt, he felt too.

At twenty-six, after years of labor and responsibility, a rare period of quiet:

> "He communed much with his Father in heaven and made tremendous progress in the mastery of his human mind." (UB 128:5.6)

Year by year, the mind was being mastered. The two worlds were being drawn together. The paradox of the middle circles was resolving, not by one side winning over the other, but by both sides learning to move as one. As the circle research frames it: "I still live in the world, but I am learning to see through it."

## Section 4: The Inner Circles

At twenty-eight, Jesus left home for the Mediterranean journey that would complete his preparation. The purpose was not study, not pilgrimage, not preaching. The purpose was something the Course would later explain as essential to the Atonement:

> "The real purpose of his trip around the Mediterranean basin was to *know men.* He came very close to hundreds of humankind on this journey. He met and loved all manner of men, rich and poor, high and low, black and white, educated and uneducated, cultured and uncultured, animalistic and spiritual, religious and irreligious, moral and immoral." (UB 129:3.8)

To know men. Not to instruct them or convert them. To know them. He traveled for nearly two years, learning how humanity lives, suffers, hopes, and loves. He did this because the work he was preparing for could not be done alone.

The Course, reflecting on this, reveals the inner logic:

> "I heard one voice because I had learned that learning is attained BY teaching. I understood that I COULD NOT ATONE FOR MYSELF ALONE." (JCIM, T 5 F 10)

He could not atone for himself alone. The Atonement requires a brother. Forgiveness requires someone to forgive. Teaching requires a student. The entire mechanism of the Course, that you learn by teaching and you are healed by healing others, was something Jesus discovered through lived experience. He went to the world because his own completion depended on encountering the Sonship.

And the connection, once made, was permanent:

> "The mind that was in me is still irresistibly drawn to every mind created by God, because God's wholeness IS the wholeness of his Son." (JCIM, T 5 F 11)

Still drawn. Not was drawn, past tense. Still drawn, present tense. The man who traveled the Mediterranean to know men is still knowing them. The Course itself is an expression of that drawing: the mind that was in Jesus reaching toward every mind, because wholeness requires the whole.

During this journey, the Urantia Book records a transformation so profound it can only be described as two minds becoming one:

> "The purely human religious experience of the Son of Man well-nigh reached the apex of attainment during this, the twenty-ninth year... the phenomenon of the making of these two minds one, the experience which the Son of Man attained in completion and finality, as an incarnated mortal of the realm, on the day of his baptism in the Jordan." (UB 129:4.2)

The making of two minds one. The material mind and the spirit Adjuster, working in closer and closer partnership through decades of effort, finally approaching the point where they function as a single consciousness. This is what the Course calls hearing one voice. It is what the Urantia Book calls Adjuster attunement. And in the language of the circles, this is the first circle: the nearest possible approach of material mind and spirit Adjuster in human experience.

The paradox at this level feels different than it did in the middle circles. The world does not disappear. It becomes translucent. The temporal is still here, but it is not heavy anymore. You live in two worlds and you understand why both statements are true: you are in the world, and you are not of it.

And this was achieved, the Urantia Book insists, not through divine shortcuts but through the same mercy ministry available to all mortals:

> "Jesus did not live his life on earth in order to set an example for all other human beings to copy. He lived this life in the flesh by the same mercy ministry that you all may live your lives on earth... You may not aspire to live his life, but you can resolve to *live your lives* even as, and by the same means that, he lived his." (UB 129:4.7)

By the same means. This is not hagiography. This is a template.

And the result, by the end of the Mediterranean journey, was staggering in its quiet completeness:

> "By the end of the twenty-ninth year Jesus of Nazareth had virtually finished the living of the life required of mortals as sojourners in the flesh. He came on earth the fullness of God to be manifest to man; he had now become well-nigh the perfection of man awaiting the occasion to become manifest to God. And he did all of this before he was thirty years of age." (UB 129:4.8)

Before he was thirty. The private life, the life no one saw or recorded at the time, was essentially complete. Everything that followed, the public ministry, the miracles, the teachings, the cross, flowed from what had already been achieved in obscurity. The Course, looking back, describes the inner purpose with remarkable simplicity:

> "My mission was simply to UNITE the Will of the Sonship WITH the Will of the Father by being aware of the Father's Will myself." (JCIM, T 8 E 4)

Simply. That word carries the weight of thirty years of preparation.

## Section 5: Completion

In the late summer of his thirtieth year, Jesus withdrew to Mount Hermon for six weeks of solitude and communion. What happened there was the culmination of everything the previous twenty-five years had built:

> "He finished the mortal task of achieving the circles of mind-understanding and personality-control. Throughout this period of communion with his heavenly Father the indwelling Adjuster also completed the assigned services. The mortal goal of this earth creature was there attained." (UB 134:8.4)

The mortal goal. Achieved the circles. This is the explicit confirmation: Jesus traversed the same seven psychic circles that every mortal traverses, and he completed them. Not by skipping levels. Not by divine intervention. By the force of his own decisions, over twenty-five years of living, the same circles described in the same Urantia Book that tells us "persons become more real as they ascend."

He had become fully real. The most real person who ever lived. And the path he took to get there used the same equipment available to every mortal on every inhabited world: a human mind, an indwelling Adjuster, and the power of decision.

Then came the baptism. And here the story takes a turn that even the celestial observers did not expect.

> "When Jesus of Nazareth went down into the Jordan to be baptized, he was a mortal of the realm who had attained the pinnacle of human evolutionary ascension in all matters related to the conquest of mind and to self-identification with the spirit." (UB 136:2.2)

The pinnacle of human evolutionary ascension. Not a God pretending to be human. A human being who had completed the entire journey, from the silent arrival of the Adjuster at age five to the full mastery of mind at age thirty. Perfect synchrony. Full communication. The mortal mind and the divine Adjuster, functioning as one.

Normally, at this level of attainment, the expected event is fusion: the permanent merger of human identity with divine spirit. The celestial observers, watching from beyond mortal sight, expected exactly this:

> "Many superhuman observers expected to witness the fusion of the Adjuster with its indwelt mind, but they were all destined to suffer disappointment. Something new and even greater occurred." (UB 136:2.3)

Instead of fusion, the Adjuster personalized. Instead of merging, it departed and returned as an independent being, the chief of its kind in the entire local universe. This was unprecedented. This was beyond the normal mortal path, beyond anything the watching universe had seen before.

The Course captures what this moment meant from the inside:

> "It is possible even in this world to hear ONLY that voice and no other. It takes effort and great willingness to learn. It is the final lesson that I learned." (JCIM, T 5 D 1)

The final lesson. Hearing only one voice. It took effort and great willingness and thirty years of decisions, but it was learned. And having learned it, he could teach it.

> "This day of baptism ended the purely human life of Jesus. The divine Son has found his Father, the Universal Father has found his incarnated Son, and they speak the one to the other." (UB 136:2.7)

The forty days that followed were not what popular tradition imagines. They were not a contest with Satan. They were the first full functioning of the unified mind:

> "The mind of man has become the mind of God from this time on, and though the selfhood of the mind of man is ever present, always does this spiritualized human mind say, 'Not my will but yours be done.'" (UB 136:4.4)

The human mind was not erased. It was present. But it had been so thoroughly aligned with the divine will that its permanent posture was surrender. Not the surrender of defeat. The surrender of a person who has become so real, so fully themselves, that there is nothing left to defend. This is what the Course calls accepting the Atonement for yourself.

> "I completed my part in it as a man, and can now complete it through other men." (JCIM, T 4 G 15)

He completed his part as a man. The circles were traversed. The mind was mastered. The human and the divine spoke as one. And the paradox that had accompanied him since the age of ten, the tension between the temporal and the eternal, between the carpenter's shop and the cosmic mission, was not resolved by choosing one side over the other. It was resolved by becoming so real that both sides were contained within a single life.

## Section 6: The Final Demonstration

Everything the previous sections have described was preparation: the slow building of a mind and a will strong enough to face what came next. The circles were completed. The mortal goal was attained. The human mind and the divine mind functioned as one. Now the question became: what does the completed person do when everything is taken away?

Begin with Gethsemane. The night is dark. The apostles are supposed to be keeping watch, but they have fallen asleep. Jesus is alone.

> "And when he had fallen down on his face, he prayed: 'My Father, I came into this world to do your will, and so have I. I know that the hour has come to lay down this life in the flesh, and I do not shrink therefrom, but I would know that it is your will that I drink this cup.'" (UB 182:3.1)

His prayer is not a plea to escape. It is a request for assurance. He is not asking God to change the plan. He is asking God to confirm that the plan still serves the purpose for which he came.

What follows is the human struggle at its most intense. The Urantia Book pulls the lens close enough to see both sides of his nature at work:

> "His human heart longed to find out whether there might not possibly be some legitimate avenue of escape from this terrible plight of suffering and sorrow. And when it had thus sought escape, and failed, it was willing to drink the cup. The divine mind of Michael knew he had done his best for the twelve apostles; but the human heart of Jesus wished that more might have been done for them before they should be left alone in the world. Jesus' heart was being crushed; he truly loved his brethren." (UB 182:3.9)

The divine mind knew. The human heart wished. Two perspectives in one person, both true, both present. The mind steady, the heart breaking. And the resolution came not by suppressing the human but by the human choosing freely:

> "Before Judas and the soldiers arrived, the Master had fully regained his customary poise; the spirit had triumphed over the flesh; faith had asserted itself over all human tendencies to fear or entertain doubt." (UB 182:3.11)

Faith triumphed. Not omniscience. Not divine power overriding mortal weakness. Faith, the same faculty available to every human being, asserting itself over the fear. This is what circle attainment looks like under pressure. Every level of growth, every year of decisions, every painful step from the seventh circle to the first, was being tested in the fire of this single night. And it held.

Now hear how Jesus himself, in the Course, looks back on this night:

> "My brothers slept during the so-called 'agony in the garden,' but I could not be angry with them, because I had learned I could not BE abandoned." (JCIM, T 6 B 8)

He could not be angry because he had learned he could not be abandoned. The Adjuster was there. The Father was there. Even when every human companion failed, the lesson he had spent thirty years learning held. He was not alone. He could not be alone. And because he knew this, the sleeping apostles were not betrayers but brothers who needed mercy.

Then the cross.

We covered the theology of the crucifixion in Chapter 14: not payment, not punishment, but demonstration. What this section offers is something different: the personal experience. What was the man on the cross thinking? What was he feeling? The Course gives us his inner voice at the most extreme moment of human existence.

> "I WAS persecuted as the world judges, and did NOT share this evaluation for myself. And because I did not share it, I did NOT strengthen it. I therefore offered a DIFFERENT interpretation of attack, and one which I DO want to share with you." (JCIM, T 6 B 5)

He was persecuted. He does not deny it. But he did not share the evaluation. He saw it and chose not to identify with it. The world called it persecution; he called it something else. And by choosing a different interpretation, he demonstrated that the interpretation we choose is the one that becomes real for us.

> "I elected, both for your sake AND mine, to demonstrate that the most outrageous assault, as judged by the ego, did not matter." (JCIM, T 6 B 10)

For your sake and mine. Not only for ours. For his own. He needed to demonstrate it to himself as much as to us. The crucifixion was not theater performed for an audience. It was a man testing the truth of everything he had learned, in the most extreme conditions imaginable, and finding that it held.

The Course says the world is a dream. Jesus, on the cross, demonstrated what it looks like when someone has woken up from the dream but is still inside it. The body is being destroyed. The person is untouched. The material is being stripped away. The real, the reality that grows with each circle traversed, remains. The Urantia Book captures the significance:

> "The great thing about the death of Jesus, as it is related to the enrichment of human experience and the enlargement of the way of salvation, is not the *fact* of his death but rather the superb manner and the matchless spirit in which he met death." (UB 188:4.12)

Not the fact. The manner. The spirit. How he held himself in the fire of everything that could be taken away, and found that the one thing that mattered could not be taken.

And then, the final proof that the body cannot contain the person who has become fully real:

> "His material or physical body was not a part of the resurrected personality. When Jesus came forth from the tomb, his body of flesh remained undisturbed in the sepulchre." (UB 189:1.7)

The body stayed behind. The person walked out. Everything the circles had built, every year of becoming more real, culminated in this: the material form was no longer needed. What was real about Jesus had never been the flesh. It was the personality, the soul, the identity forged through thirty years of decisions, and that identity simply continued, in a form beyond the physical, exactly as the circles had promised.

> "Touch me not, Mary, for I am not as you knew me in the flesh. In this form will I tarry with you for a season before I ascend to the Father." (UB 189:4.12)

I am not as you knew me in the flesh. But I am still me. The same person. More real, not less. The resurrection was not the creation of something new. It was the revelation of what had been growing all along, from the silent arrival of the Adjuster at age five to the final circle completed on Mount Hermon. The person who emerged from the tomb was the same person who had been becoming more real for thirty years. The body was the last shadow to fall away.

The Course, reflecting on this, makes a promise that extends the circle journey to every person who has ever lived:

> "I am raised in the form that you also shall have when you depart from this world." (UB 191:5.4)

The form that you also shall have. The circles do not lead to a destination reserved for Jesus alone. They lead to the same destination for everyone. The morontia form, the resurrection body, the life beyond the flesh, is universal. His journey proves that the path works. His resurrection proves where it leads.

## Section 7: Closing

There is one more voice to hear in this story. Not Jesus speaking. Not the Urantia Book's historians. A different voice entirely.

The Adjuster that indwelt Jesus for thirty years, the divine fragment that arrived silently in the mind of a five-year-old boy and accompanied him through every prayer, every decision, every loss, every victory, left behind a testimony. A Solitary Messenger recorded it:

> "This same Adjuster now reflects in the inscrutable nature of his mighty personality the prebaptismal humanity of Joshua ben Joseph, the eternal and living transcript of the eternal and living values which the greatest of all Urantians created out of the humble circumstances of a commonplace life as it was lived to the complete exhaustion of the spiritual values attainable in mortal experience." (UB 109:6.6)

The eternal and living transcript. Every value Jesus created during his thirty human years is preserved forever in the personality of the Adjuster that shared his mind. Not lost. Not forgotten. An eternal record of what one mortal life can achieve when the circles are traversed to completion.

And that life, the Urantia Book insists, was commonplace. Not extraordinary in its circumstances. A carpenter's son in a small town. A bereaved family. Manual labor, poverty, grief, loneliness. A commonplace life, lived to the complete exhaustion of the spiritual values attainable in mortal experience. This is what becoming fully real looks like from the outside: ordinary. The extraordinary was happening within.

The Course confirms this from the other direction. Jesus does not claim to be fundamentally different from anyone reading his words:

> "There is nothing about me that you cannot attain. I have nothing that does not come from God. The main difference between us as yet is that I have NOTHING ELSE." (JCIM, T 1 B 40y)

Nothing about him that you cannot attain. Not because he was less than people believe, but because you are more. The spark in you is the same spark that was in him. The Adjuster in your mind is the same kind that was in his. The means available to you, knowledge, experience, wisdom, decision, are the same means he used. The difference is not in kind. It is in completion. He finished. You are still walking.

But the path is the same. And the one who walked it first has not left you to walk it alone:

> "It is only my own devotion that entitles me to yours. But you will notice that I have knelt at your altar as readily as I would ever have you kneel at mine." (JCIM, T 2 B 70)

He knelt at your altar. The one who completed the entire journey, who exhausted the spiritual values of mortal experience, who traversed every circle and stood in the fire and found that love holds, this one has knelt at your altar. Not above you. Beside you.

> "I cannot forget my need to teach what I have learned which arose in me BECAUSE I learned it. I call upon you to teach what you have learned, because by so doing YOU can depend on it." (JCIM, T 5 F 13)

He learned it. In the carpenter's shop and at the temple. In the years of silence and the years of clarity. In the garden and on the cross. He learned it, and the learning created in him a compulsion to teach that cannot be forgotten and will not be withdrawn.

And the teaching, distilled to its essence, is this: you are becoming more real. Every decision you make toward the light, every moment you choose the Father's will over the ego's demands, every time you forgive when condemnation would be easier, you traverse another fraction of the circles. You become more genuinely yourself. More solid. More present. More fully what a person can be.

The Course says you are at home in God, dreaming of exile. The Urantia Book maps the waking-up process, circle by circle, and shows you that even the one who walked it first had to traverse every level, had to endure the gap between knowing and hearing, had to face tragedy and temptation and loneliness with nothing but the same equipment you carry.

He became more real, year by year, until he was the most real person who ever lived. And then he left a Course so that we could do the same.

> "The struggling and valiant human Jesus of Galilee, who, by the valor of his personal religious faith and the heroism of his indwelling Adjuster, ascended from the lowly levels of humanity to become one with divinity, thus becoming the new and living way whereby all mortals may so ascend from humanity to divinity." (UB 196:2.4)

The new and living way. Not his way alone. Yours.
