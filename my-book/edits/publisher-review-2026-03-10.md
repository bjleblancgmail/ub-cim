# Publisher's Review: *Fusion: Two Revelations, One Path*

**Manuscript by Joseph**
**Review date: March 10, 2026**

---

## Overall Assessment

This is a genuinely original work of comparative theology. The central thesis, that A Course in Miracles is a course in what the Urantia Book calls Adjuster fusion, is compelling, well-argued, and, to my knowledge, has never been articulated this clearly before. The book has a clear audience (readers of either or both texts), a consistent authorial voice, and a structural logic that builds across chapters. It is not a casual survey; it is serious scholarship presented in accessible prose.

**The manuscript is publishable.** What follows are the issues that would need to be addressed before it is ready.

---

## I. STRENGTHS

**1. The thesis is clear and original.** Chapter 3 is the heart of the book, and it delivers. The three Course quotes using "fuse/fusing," paired with the UB's fusion theology, create a genuine insight. The reframing of "Not my will but yours be done" as "It is my will that your will be done" is memorable and could become the book's signature idea.

**2. Source balance is excellent.** Across the full manuscript, UB and Course quotes appear in roughly equal numbers (~90 each). Neither community will feel the book belongs exclusively to the other side.

**3. The direct addresses work.** The introduction addresses three audiences (UB readers, Course readers, newcomers) without condescension. Chapter 1, Section 7 ("A Word to Each Community") is one of the strongest passages in the manuscript, stacking five UB quotes about material unreality before re-reading the Course's opening lines.

**4. Intellectual honesty.** Chapter 8 (What Survives) openly names the one genuine difference between the texts (permanent refusal vs. inevitable return). Chapter 24 of Appendix C admits "no clean parallel" exists. This kind of honesty builds reader trust.

**5. The scaffolding passage.** Chapter 7, Section 7 ("Serviceable Scaffolding") is the best single passage in the book. "Do not despise your body. It carried you." This is the voice of a writer, not a theologian.

## II. STRUCTURAL ISSUES

**1. Chapter length variance is extreme.**

| Chapter | Words |
|---------|-------|
| Ch 6 (Forgiveness) | ~2,000 |
| Ch 12 (From Here to Paradise) | ~2,400 |
| Ch 11 (Atonement) | ~2,600 |
| Ch 1 (Two Revelations) | ~2,800 |
| Ch 10 (Lucifer Rebellion) | ~3,200 |
| Ch 8 (What Survives) | ~3,250 |
| Ch 2 (Terminology Bridge) | ~3,500 |
| Ch 5 (Miracles and Time) | ~3,700 |
| Ch 9 (Architecture of Mind) | ~3,800 |
| Ch 4 (Sonship/Supreme) | ~3,200 |
| Ch 3 (Central Thesis) | ~4,200 |
| Ch 7 (What Is Real) | ~5,100 |
| Ch 13 (Jesus Both Books Reveal) | ~6,500 |

Chapter 13 is more than triple Chapter 6's length. Chapters 6, 11, and 12 feel underdeveloped relative to their subjects. A publisher would expect chapters to fall within a tighter range (3,000-5,000 words each). Consider expanding Chapters 6, 11, and 12, or trimming Chapter 13's seven-bestowal section.

**2. Chapter 8 has no closing section.** Every other chapter ends with a "Closing" that ties back to the fusion thesis. Chapter 8 simply stops after naming the one genuine difference between the texts. It needs a resolution or framing sentence.

**3. Chapter 3 has two closings.** Sections 11 ("Closing") and 12 ("Map and Practice") are both endings. Merge or cut one.

**4. Chapter 12 leans heavily UB with almost no Course voice.** Sections 5 and 6 are pure UB cosmology summary with only 4 total quotes in the whole chapter. The book's premise (the Course prepares, the UB maps the road) explains this, but the imbalance is still jarring after 11 chapters of careful pairing.

**5. Chapter 1, Section 4 (Editing History) reads like an appendix.** The four-version history of the Course is informative but slows the opening momentum. Consider moving it to an endnote or appendix, or condensing to one paragraph.

## III. REPETITION

This is the most significant editorial issue in the manuscript.

**Repeated quotes across chapters:**
- UB 107:1.2 ("They are of God...") appears in Ch 2 and Ch 3
- ACIM 1.I.52 (Soul in grace) appears in Ch 2 and Ch 3
- ACIM 1.I.68 (Soul unalterable) appears in Ch 2 and Ch 3
- UB 188:5.2 (divine love destroys sin) appears 3 times in Ch 6 and again in Ch 11
- UB 188:4.12 (the great thing about the death of Jesus) appears in Ch 11 and Ch 13
- ACIM 1.I.13 (miracle as beginning and ending) appears in Ch 5 and Ch 8
- UB 151:3.3 appears twice within Ch 7 itself

**Repeated metaphors:**
- The seed/flower metaphor appears three times (Ch 2 twice, Ch 3 once)
- Jesus' baptism/fusion is told twice in Ch 12 (Sections 2 and 4)

**Recommendation:** A key quote can appear twice if it serves a genuinely different argument. But verbatim repetition of the same quote + the same commentary erodes the reader's confidence. Do a deduplication pass. Present the quote fully the first time; reference it by shorthand subsequently ("As we saw in Chapter 2...").

## IV. ERRORS AND INCONSISTENCIES

**Typos:**
- Introduction line 45: "clairty" -> "clarity"
- Ch 1 line 47: "paralles" -> "parallels," "stiking" -> "striking," "Urantia book" -> "Urantia Book"
- Ch 7 line 281: "The Course say:" -> "The Course says:"
- Ch 7 line 159: Missing space before "The empty shell."

**Misattribution:**
- **Ch 11 line 87:** A UB quote (UB 188:5.2, "Divine love does not merely forgive wrongs; it absorbs and actually destroys them") is introduced with "The Course says:" This must be corrected.

**Citation discrepancy:**
- The "wills fuse" quote is cited as `acim oe txt 8.V.36` in Chapter 3 and `acim oe txt 8.V.15` in Chapter 4. One is wrong. Verify against the OE source.

**Formatting inconsistencies:**
- Ch 8: Several Course quotes lack blockquote `>` markers (lines 17-18, 21-22, 75-76)
- Ch 9: Judas passage uses ALL CAPS for emphasis while the rest of the book uses italics
- Ch 10 line 35: A UB quote is presented as regular text without blockquote formatting
- Appendix B entry #7: Drops the standard quote formatting convention

**Missing STATUS line:** Chapter 3 is the only chapter without a STATUS marker.

---

## V. APPENDICES

**Appendix A (Terminology Mapping):**
- Three terms in the quick-reference table (Real, Transfer, Unreal) have no corresponding body paragraphs. Write them or remove them from the table.
- No OE citations on the Course quotes. Given the CIMS permission requirements, citations should be added.

**Appendix B (Selected Parallels):**
- 57 entries, well-organized. Entry #7 has nonstandard formatting that should be fixed.
- Ends abruptly with no closing statement.

**Appendix C (Miracle Principles):**
- Only covers 25 of 50 principles with no note about the remaining 25. Either complete or state the scope explicitly.
- Numbering diverges from the Course's own numbering starting at Principle 22 (where the appendix combines principles 22-24 into one entry). This will confuse readers who look up the originals.
- Ends abruptly with no closing.

---

## VI. VOICE AND PROSE

The prose is clean and confident. The author avoids academic jargon without dumbing down the content. The connective tissue between quotes, the author's own voice explaining what the reader just read, is where the book lives or dies, and it lives. The author trusts the reader without hand-holding.

Two notes:
- Chapter 5, Section 2 has four consecutive Course quotes introduced with nearly identical lead-ins ("The Course says:", "And the Course says:", "The Course also says:"). Vary the transitions.
- Chapter 10 includes a paragraph about Helen Schucman's scribing process (Section 2) that belongs in Chapter 1, not in a chapter about the Lucifer rebellion.

---

## VII. WHAT A PUBLISHER WOULD ASK

1. **Who is the primary audience?** The manuscript currently serves both communities equally. A publisher will want to know: is this primarily for Course readers discovering the UB, or UB readers discovering the Course? The answer affects marketing, cover design, and shelf placement.

2. **What about the other 25 Miracle Principles?** Appendix C's incompleteness looks like an unfinished manuscript. Complete it or scope it explicitly.

3. **Foreword or endorsement.** A book bridging two communities needs credibility in both. A foreword from a respected voice in either the UB or ACIM community would significantly strengthen the book's reception.

4. **Title and subtitle are strong.** "Fusion" works on multiple levels (the theological concept, the bridging of two communities). No change needed.

---

## VIII. RECOMMENDED EDITORIAL PRIORITIES

In order of importance:

1. **Fix the misattribution in Ch 11** (UB quote labeled as Course)
2. **Verify the 8.V.36 vs 8.V.15 citation** for the "wills fuse" quote
3. **Fix all typos** (4 identified)
4. **Deduplication pass** across the full manuscript
5. **Add a closing section to Ch 8**
6. **Merge Ch 3's two closings**
7. **Standardize blockquote formatting** in Ch 8 and Ch 10
8. **Expand Ch 6 and Ch 12** or accept the length variance
9. **Complete or scope Appendix C**
10. **Add OE citations to Appendix A**
11. **Fix Appendix A table/body mismatch**
12. **Add closings to Appendices B and C**

---

## Bottom Line

The thesis is original. The evidence is substantial. The voice is authentic. The book has real value for both communities and for readers encountering these texts for the first time. The issues above are normal for a manuscript at this stage: they are editorial, not conceptual. The architecture is sound. The foundation holds.

This book deserves to be finished well.
