# Chapter 17 Cross-Reference: Overlaps with Chapters 6, 14, and 15

**Purpose:** Identify UB and CIM quotes from the Ch 17 plan that already appear in earlier chapters, and suggest how Ch 17 can use them differently (or avoid them).

---

## UB QUOTES

### Paper 182 (Gethsemane) -- OVERLAP FOUND in Ch 6

**Chapter 6, Section 6 ("The Bridge"), lines 205-209:**
> "I am the living bridge from one world to another." (UB 182:1.24)
> "I am the living link between time and eternity." (UB 182:1.25)

**Framing in Ch 6:** Theological/structural. Used to establish Jesus as the bridge between time and eternity, paired with CIM's Holy Spirit as "the bridge or thought-transfer of perception to knowledge." The focus is on the bridge mechanism, not on Gethsemane as a human moment.

**Suggestion for Ch 17:** If Ch 17 uses Paper 182, focus on the *human struggle* of Gethsemane: Jesus' anguish, the loneliness, the garden itself as a scene. Ch 6 used the "bridge" lines abstractly. Ch 17 can use the same paper but draw from the narrative passages (Jesus praying alone, the apostles sleeping, "this cup") rather than the bridge metaphor. If Ch 17 does want to reference the bridge lines, a brief callback ("As we saw in Chapter 6, Jesus called himself the living bridge") is cleaner than re-quoting.

### Paper 188 (Crucifixion) -- OVERLAP FOUND in Ch 14

**Chapter 14, Section 2 ("What the Crucifixion Was Not"), line 19:**
> "The death on the cross was not to effect man's reconciliation to God but to stimulate man's realization of the Father's eternal love and his Son's unending mercy." (UB 188:5.13)

**Chapter 14, Section 2, line 27:**
> "This entire idea of the ransom of the atonement places salvation upon a plane of unreality." (UB 188:4.13)

**Chapter 14, Section 2, lines 23-25 (from full text read):**
- UB 188:4.8 ("utterly abandon all those primitive notions about God as an offended monarch")
- UB 188:4.3 ("The Father in heaven never conceived of such crass injustice")
- UB 188:4.11 ("he certainly would not be satisfied with the childish scheme of substituting an innocent sufferer")
- UB 188:4.10 ("True, wise fathers may chasten their sons, but they do all this in love")

**Chapter 14, Section 3 ("What the Crucifixion Was"), line 39:**
- UB 188:4.12 ("the superb manner and the matchless spirit in which he met death")
- UB 188:5.9 ("a sacred symbol of the good bestowing themselves upon the evil")
- UB 188:5.2 ("Divine love does not merely forgive wrongs; it absorbs and actually destroys them")

**Framing in Ch 14:** Theological correction. Ch 14 uses Paper 188 extensively to dismantle substitutionary atonement. The quotes serve a doctrinal purpose: what the cross was NOT (payment) and what it WAS (demonstration of love).

**Suggestion for Ch 17:** Paper 188 is heavily used in Ch 14. If Ch 17 needs the crucifixion, focus on what Paper 188 reveals about Jesus' *inner experience* during the cross (if that is part of the Ch 17 plan), or reference Ch 14 for the theological framework and use Paper 188 only for any quotes not already cited. The "superb manner" and "absorbs and destroys" quotes are already deployed. Do not re-quote them.

### Paper 136 (Baptism, forty days) -- PARTIAL OVERLAP in Ch 15

**Chapter 15, Section 2 ("The Goal: Fusion"), lines 27-28:**
"Jesus demonstrated this. At his baptism, the Urantia Book records that he had achieved such complete harmony with his Adjuster that fusion could have occurred."

**Chapter 15, Section 4 ("Jesus: The Pattern of Progression"), lines 55-56:**
"At his baptism in the Jordan, this process reached its culmination. The Urantia Book records that Jesus had achieved such complete synchrony with his Adjuster that fusion could have occurred, the permanent union of human identity with divine spirit."

**Framing in Ch 15:** Summary/narrative. Ch 15 references the baptism as a milestone in the ascension path, but does NOT directly quote Paper 136. It paraphrases the achievement ("such complete harmony/synchrony with his Adjuster that fusion could have occurred") without quoting specific verses. No forty days material is used.

**Suggestion for Ch 17:** The baptism is mentioned twice in Ch 15 but only in general terms. Ch 17 can quote Paper 136 directly (specific verses about the baptism experience, the forty days in the wilderness, the decisions Jesus made) without duplicating Ch 15. The key is to go deeper: Ch 15 used the baptism as a waypoint on the ascension map. Ch 17 can use it as a window into Jesus' inner experience of mind mastery. Quote the actual text; Ch 15 only paraphrased.

## CIM QUOTES

### T 6 B 5-16 (Crucifixion reflections) -- SIGNIFICANT OVERLAP in Ch 14

**Chapter 14, Section 3 ("What the Crucifixion Was"), lines 33-41:**
- Tx 6.4: "nothing more than an extreme example" whose value "lies solely in the kind of learning it facilitates"
- Chapter 6 block quote: "Assault can ultimately be made only on the body... the message which the crucifixion was intended to teach was that it is not necessary to perceive any form of assault in persecution because you cannot be persecuted."

If Ch 17's plan for "all crucifixion reflections" from T 6 B 5-16 means quoting specific verses from this section, check carefully which ones Ch 14 already used. The three quoted above are deployed. Other verses from T 6 B 5-16 that Ch 14 did NOT use could still be brought in, especially any that focus on Jesus' *personal experience* of the crucifixion rather than its theological meaning. "Teach only love" should NOT be re-quoted in Ch 17; it is already a climactic moment in Ch 14.

### CIM Chapter 5 "model for decision" -- OVERLAP in Ch 6

**Chapter 6, Section 11 ("Closing"), lines 433-435:**
> "My mind will always be like yours, because we were created as equals. It was only my decision that gave me all power in heaven and earth. My only gift to you is to help you make the same decision for yourself... I am your model for decision. By deciding for God, I showed you that this decision can be made and that you can make it." (JCIM, Chapter 5)

**Framing in Ch 6:** Used as the closing argument. The "model for decision" quote is the chapter's final CIM citation, paired with the UB's "follow him along this same experience of Father discovery." It is a climactic moment.

If Ch 17 needs to reference it, use a callback ("As Jesus says in what we read in Chapter 6, he is our model for decision") rather than a full re-quote. If Ch 17 has a different angle on it (e.g., what the "decision" actually looked like in Jesus' daily human experience), a brief excerpt rather than the full block could work.

## KEY FINDINGS

**1. The majority of Ch 17's planned material is CLEAR.** Papers 123-129, 134:8, 109:6, 196:2, and 110:6 are untouched. CIM T 4 B 11 and T 2 B 70 are completely fresh. This is good news: the biographical/narrative Jesus material (childhood through baptism) is virgin territory.

**2. Paper 188 is the biggest overlap risk.** Ch 14 quotes it extensively (at least 7 specific verses). Ch 17 should not re-quote Paper 188 material. If the crucifixion needs to appear in Ch 17, reference Ch 14 and use any Paper 188 verses that Ch 14 skipped.

**3. T 6 B material needs per-verse checking.** Ch 14 uses "extreme example" (Tx 6.4), the assault/persecution passage, "Teach only love" (Tx 6.17-18), and "the separation never occurred." Other T 6 B verses (5-16 is a range) may be available, especially those focused on Jesus' personal experience rather than theological correction.

**4. Paper 182 can be reused with a different lens.** Ch 6 used the "living bridge" lines as metaphor. Ch 17 can use Gethsemane as narrative/emotional scene (the garden, the prayer, the human struggle) without touching the bridge metaphor.

**5. Paper 136 (Baptism) is available for direct quoting.** Ch 15 only paraphrased; it never quoted specific verses from Paper 136. Ch 17 has full freedom to quote the baptism text directly.

**6. The "model for decision" quote (CIM Ch 5) should not be re-quoted.** It is Ch 6's closing capstone. A callback is fine.

**7. The T 5 F "heard one voice" and "need to teach" material is completely untouched.** This is fresh, powerful, and directly relevant to a chapter about Jesus' inner experience.
